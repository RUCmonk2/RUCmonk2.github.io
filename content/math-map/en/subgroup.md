## A self-contained selection of operations

A subgroup selects elements of an existing group while keeping the same operation. The selected operations must still compose, undo one another, and include the identity. An arbitrary subset does not suffice.

## Notation

| Symbol | Meaning | Convention |
| --- | --- | --- |
| $G$ | Original group | Its axioms are already known |
| $H\le G$ | Subgroup relation | Not a numerical inequality |
| $e,h^{-1}$ | Identity and inverse | Inherited from the original group |
| $\langle g\rangle$ | Subgroup generated by $g$ | Brackets do not mean inner product here |
| $gH$ | Left coset | Left-multiply every element by $g$ |
| $[G:H]$ | Index | Number of left cosets |

A nonempty subset is a subgroup if it is closed under products and inverses. Equivalently, it is enough that $ab^{-1}\in H$ for every $a,b\in H$.

## Addition modulo six

Take $G=\{0,1,2,3,4,5\}$ with addition modulo six and $H=\{0,2,4\}$.

Zero is present. Two plus two is four, two plus four is zero, and four plus four is two. The inverse of two is four and conversely. Thus $H$ is a subgroup, generated by two.

The smaller set $\{0,2\}$ fails because it omits the result of adding two to itself.

## Cosets partition the group

Here $H=\{0,2,4\}$ and $1+H=\{1,3,5\}$ are disjoint blocks of size three covering the group. The index is two.

For finite groups,
$$
|G|=[G:H]\,|H|.
$$
A group of order six cannot have a subgroup of order four. Divisibility is necessary; it does not generally guarantee a subgroup for every divisor.

A coset need not be a subgroup: $\{1,3,5\}$ does not contain the identity.

## Normality is an additional condition

To multiply cosets without depending on representative choices, require $gHg^{-1}=H$ for every $g\in G$. Such a subgroup is normal and permits a quotient group. Every subgroup of an abelian group is normal, but noncommutative groups require a separate check.

## Exercises

1. Find the subgroup generated by two under addition modulo eight, and its index.
2. Are the even integers and the positive integers subgroups of the additive integers?

### Exercise 1

The subgroup is $\{0,2,4,6\}$ and its index is two.

### Exercise 2

Even integers contain zero and are closed under addition and negation. Positive integers omit zero and additive inverses. Including zero still leaves missing inverses.

## Tests, cosets, and structural constructions

The nonempty $ab^{-1}$ test recovers identity, inverses, and product closure; associativity is inherited. Intersections of subgroups remain subgroups, defining the subgroup generated by any subset. Unions generally fail closure.

Left cosets are equal or disjoint and each is bijective with the subgroup, yielding Lagrange's theorem for finite groups. Infinite groups can have finite index without treating infinite cardinalities as ordinary numerical ratios.

Normality is equivalent to equality of left and right cosets and makes quotient multiplication well-defined. Stabilizers from group actions are subgroups; kernels of homomorphisms are normal subgroups. The orbit–stabilizer formula connects subgroup indices to action orbit sizes. Divisibility alone is not a general existence theorem for every subgroup order.
